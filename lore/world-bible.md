# Into the Void - World Bible

> *"We didn't find Terminus. Terminus was waiting for us."*
> — Dr. Yuki Okonkwo, First Survey Team, 3012 CE

---

## Part I: The State of Humanity

### The Collapse (circa 2400-2600 CE)

No one alive remembers what truly ended Earth. The records are fragmented, contradictory, classified, or simply lost. Some archives speak of resource wars. Others mention environmental cascade failures. A few encrypted files reference something called "The Silence" — but their contents were never recovered.

What is known: Earth is gone. Not destroyed — just... empty. The last probe to return from Sol showed cities standing, oceans still blue, forests still green. But no signals. No life signs. No explanation.

Humanity doesn't speak of Earth anymore. It's considered bad luck.

### The Scattering (2600-3000 CE)

Humanity survived in fragments. Generation ships. Orbital stations. Hastily terraformed moons. Mining outposts that became permanent by necessity. Twelve major colonies emerged, connected by months-long trade routes and years-long communication delays.

Each colony developed independently. Cultures diverged. Languages shifted. But one thing remained constant: resources were finite, populations grew, and the colonies were dying — slowly, but certainly.

By 3000 CE, the Twelve Colonies had become the Eight. Then the Six.

The remaining colonies formed the **Intercolonial Corporate Council (ICC)** — not a government, but a regulatory body for the megacorporations that had become the true powers. The ICC's mandate was simple: find a solution, or manage extinction.

### The Discovery (2987 CE)

The long-range probe *Orpheus-7* was one of thousands sent into unexplored space over centuries. Most never returned. *Orpheus-7* was different.

It found a planet in a region of space so empty it had been marked on charts simply as "The Void" — a starless gap between spiral arms where nothing should exist. And yet, there it was: a world with breathable atmosphere, liquid water, and — impossibly — ruins.

Someone had been there before.

The ICC classified the discovery immediately. For twenty-five years, they planned in secret. This would not be a rushed colonization. This would be humanity's last, best hope.

They named it **Terminus**. The end of the journey.

### The Expedition (3012 CE)

The **Terminus Colonization Initiative** launched with one million colonists drawn from all surviving colonies. Three megacorporations pooled resources to make it possible, each securing rights to a territory on the new world.

The journey took fourteen months.

What the colonists found exceeded all projections — and all fears. Terminus was rich beyond imagination: rare minerals, unknown biological compounds, energy signatures that defied current physics. The ruins were vast, alien, and utterly silent.

And sometimes, in the deep places, things moved.

## Part II: The Planet Terminus

### Overview

Terminus orbits a stable yellow star in a region of space nearly devoid of other stellar bodies. Why a habitable world exists in such isolation remains one of the planet's many mysteries.

**Basic Data:**
- Gravity: 0.94 Earth standard
- Atmosphere: Nitrogen-oxygen mix (breathable with minor filtration)
- Day length: 27.3 standard hours
- Year length: 412 local days
- Moons: Two — *Vigil* (large, tidally locked) and *Whisper* (small, erratic orbit)

### Biomes

Terminus exhibits remarkable environmental diversity across ten distinct biome types. Due to the planet's unusual geological and climatological patterns, these biomes appear in seemingly random distribution — a temperate forest may border a crystalline waste with no transitional zone. Xenogeologists theorize this patchwork may not be natural.

Each biome hosts adapted variants of Terminus's flora and fauna, distinct resource profiles, and unique hazards.

#### Survival Tiers

Corporate orientation classifies biomes into four survival tiers based on equipment requirements and baseline danger levels. Higher-tier biomes offer proportionally greater resource yields and discovery bonuses — risk and reward scale together.

| Tier | Classification | Requirements | Profit Multiplier |
|------|---------------|--------------|-------------------|
| I | **Frontier** | Standard issue gear | 1.0x (baseline) |
| II | **Hazardous** | Specialized equipment, field experience | 1.5-2.0x |
| III | **Hostile** | Advanced gear, survival training, team support | 2.5-3.5x |
| IV | **Extreme** | Elite equipment, extensive experience, corporate authorization | 4.0-6.0x |

New arrivals are restricted to Tier I biomes until demonstrating competency. Attempting higher-tier zones without proper preparation is not forbidden — the corporations simply don't recover bodies from unauthorized expeditions.

#### 1. Luminous Canopy

> *The forests that never sleep.*

**Survival Tier: I (Frontier)** — Manageable with standard equipment. Recommended starting biome for Verdant-affiliated arrivals.

**Environment:** Dense temperate rainforest dominated by towering fungal-trees and interwoven canopy layers. Bioluminescence saturates every surface — leaves pulse with soft light, bark glows in patterns, and the forest floor shimmers with phosphorescent moss. Nights here are brighter than days, painted in blues, greens, and purples.

**Flora:** Giant fungal trees (80-200m), luminous vine networks, spore-releasing understory plants, symbiotic moss colonies. Many plants respond to proximity — brightening when approached, dimming when threatened.

**Fauna:** Predominantly herbivores and omnivores adapted to three-dimensional canopy life. Predators here are ambush specialists, using bioluminescent mimicry to lure prey. Maniacs are rare but devastating when they appear — their rampage through interconnected ecosystems causes cascade failures.

**Resources:** Biocompounds for medicine and bioengineering, luminescent materials, rare fungal extracts, living construction material. *(Common materials, moderate value)*

**Hazards:** Disorientation from constant light, toxic spore clouds, territorial canopy predators, symbiotic plant traps.

**Atmosphere:** Ethereal, alive, watchful. The forest feels aware. Many colonists report the sensation of being observed — some attribute this to wildlife, others aren't sure.

---

#### 2. Volcanic Reaches

> *Where the planet bleeds.*

**Survival Tier: III (Hostile)** — Requires thermal protection systems, heat-resistant equipment, and emergency protocols. High-value extraction zone for experienced crews.

**Environment:** Active geothermal zones with exposed lava flows, obsidian fields, ash plains, and steam vents. The ground is hot enough to burn through standard footwear in some areas. The air shimmers with heat distortion, and the sky is often hazy with volcanic particulates.

**Flora:** Extremophile plants with metalite root systems that tap geothermal energy directly. Crystalline growths that may or may not be alive. Heat-resistant fungal mats surrounding thermal vents.

**Fauna:** Silicon-heavy organisms that thrive in extreme temperatures. Herbivores graze on mineral deposits and thermal vent bacteria. Predators here are armored, slow, and devastating — ambush hunters that wait in obsidian fields for days. Maniacs are more common near unstable zones, possibly driven mad by seismic vibrations.

**Resources:** Rich mineral deposits (iron, copper, rare metals), geothermal energy sources, volcanic glass for tools, heat-resistant materials. *(High-value minerals, excellent yields)*

**Hazards:** Extreme temperatures, sudden eruptions, lava tube collapses, toxic gas vents, aggressive silicon-armored predators.

**Atmosphere:** Harsh, primal, honest. The Volcanic Reaches don't pretend to be hospitable. Everything here is trying to kill you, and it's obvious about it.

---

#### 3. Crystalline Wastes

> *Beauty that cuts.*

**Survival Tier: III (Hostile)** — Requires reinforced protective gear, navigation systems, water reserves, and anti-disorientation training. Ancient artifact hotspot.

**Environment:** Vast plains and valleys dominated by silicon-crystal formations ranging from small clusters to towering spires hundreds of meters tall. Light refracts through the crystals creating rainbow patterns and false images. The terrain is sharp, reflective, and acoustically strange — sounds echo unpredictably.

**Flora:** Minimal. What exists is silicon-based: crystal-plants that grow imperceptibly slowly, lattice-moss that spreads across formation surfaces, and occasional parasitic organisms that draw energy from the crystals themselves.

**Fauna:** Sparse but highly specialized. Creatures here have crystalline integuments, reflective camouflage, or echolocation adapted to the acoustic chaos. Predators use light manipulation to disorient prey. The rare maniacs in this biome are terrifying — their rampages shatter crystal formations, creating deadly shard-storms.

**Resources:** High-purity silicon crystals, rare prismatic minerals, resonant materials for technology applications, Ancient artifacts (ruins often found partially absorbed by crystal growth). *(Rare materials, high tech value)*

**Hazards:** Razor-sharp terrain, disorienting reflections, acoustic distortion, shard-storms, dehydration (little water available).

**Atmosphere:** Alien, mesmerizing, treacherous. The Crystalline Wastes are the most visually stunning biome on Terminus — and among the most lethal. Many colonists who enter become transfixed by the beauty. Some never leave.

---

#### 4. Fungal Depths

> *Below the world, another world.*

**Survival Tier: III (Hostile)** — Requires respiratory filtration, anti-parasitic protocols, navigation beacons, and emergency oxygen. Pharmaceutical goldmine for prepared teams.

**Environment:** Massive underground cavern systems dominated by fungal ecosystems that dwarf their surface counterparts. Mushroom forests with caps spanning fifty meters. Mycelial networks that form living floors, walls, and ceilings. Bioluminescence ranges from soft ambient glow to blinding strobes in some species.

**Flora:** Exclusively fungal — giant shelf mushrooms forming platforms, thread-thin mycelium creating web-like barriers, puffball clusters that release spore clouds when disturbed, predatory fungi that digest organic matter on contact.

**Fauna:** Adapted to complete darkness and fungal diets. Many creatures are blind, relying on chemical sensing or vibration detection. Herbivores feed directly on fungal matter. Predators are patient — in the unchanging cavern environment, ambush hunting is the dominant strategy. Maniacs here are often fungally infected, their behavior altered by parasitic organisms.

**Resources:** Unique pharmaceutical compounds, decomposition enzymes, bioluminescent materials, mycelial fibers (incredibly strong), occasional Ancient installations built into cavern walls. *(High-value pharmaceuticals, rare fibers)*

**Hazards:** Spore inhalation (hallucinogenic to fatal), predatory fungi, cave-ins, getting lost (no GPS, no landmarks), parasitic infection, oxygen-depleted zones.

**Atmosphere:** Claustrophobic, disorienting, alive. The Fungal Depths feel like being inside a living organism — because, in some sense, you are. The entire biome may be a single interconnected mycelial network.

---

#### 5. Coastal Shallows

> *Where tides reveal secrets.*

**Survival Tier: I (Frontier)** — Manageable with standard equipment and tidal awareness. Recommended starting biome for Nexus-affiliated arrivals.

**Environment:** Extensive coastal zones with tidal flats, mangrove-equivalent forests, bioluminescent lagoons, and shallow reef systems. The dual moons create complex tidal patterns — areas submerged for hours can become exposed for days depending on orbital alignments.

**Flora:** Amphibious plants adapted to regular inundation. Filter-feeding sessile organisms that blur the plant/animal distinction. Kelp-equivalent forests in deeper waters. Tidal-zone species that open during submersion and seal during exposure.

**Fauna:** Highly diverse — terrestrial, aquatic, and amphibious species intermingle. Herbivores follow tidal patterns, grazing exposed zones during low tide. Omnivores are opportunistic scavengers of tidal debris. Predators often cross the water-land boundary, comfortable in both. Maniacs are rare but include terrifying deep-water specimens that occasionally surge into shallows.

**Resources:** Marine compounds, filtration organisms (water purification), shell materials, tidal energy potential, coral-equivalent construction material, abundant protein sources. *(Abundant common materials, food sources)*

**Hazards:** Drowning (tidal zones shift), aquatic predators, toxic marine life, storms, strong currents in narrow channels.

**Atmosphere:** Dynamic, rhythmic, abundant. The Coastal Shallows feel alive in a different way than other biomes — life here is visible, active, constantly moving. There's always something to see, catch, or avoid.

---

#### 6. Miasma Marshes

> *The air itself is the enemy.*

**Survival Tier: II (Hazardous)** — Requires chemical filtration systems, sealed gear, and decontamination protocols. Valuable chemical extraction zone.

**Environment:** Toxic wetlands where organic decomposition has run wild. Standing water ranges from ankle-deep to unfathomable. The air is thick with chemical vapors — some merely unpleasant, others lethal. Visibility is limited by perpetual haze. The ground is unreliable; what appears solid may be floating vegetation over bottomless muck.

**Flora:** Chemically aggressive plants that have evolved to dominate through toxicity. Gas-releasing pods, acid-secreting leaves, narcotic pollen clouds. Some plants are mobile, slowly repositioning to optimal conditions. Decomposer organisms are everywhere, breaking down anything organic that stops moving.

**Fauna:** Toxin-resistant specialists. Herbivores have developed immunity to specific plant poisons and feed exclusively on those species. Omnivores are scavengers consuming already-dead matter. Predators use the toxic environment as a weapon — chasing prey until chemical exposure weakens them. Maniacs may be common, but it's hard to distinguish chemically-altered behavior from baseline aggression.

**Resources:** Pharmaceutical precursors, industrial chemicals, toxins (for processing or weaponization), biogas for energy, rare compounds formed only in this chemical soup. *(Valuable chemicals, industrial applications)*

**Hazards:** Toxic air (filtration mandatory), unstable ground, chemical burns, hallucinogenic exposure, aggressive flora, poor visibility.

**Atmosphere:** Oppressive, nauseating, valuable. Nobody wants to work in the Miasma Marshes. The resources make it necessary. Every expedition feels like a countdown — how long until your filters fail?

---

#### 7. Petrified Expanse

> *A forest of stone where time forgot.*

**Survival Tier: II (Hazardous)** — Requires mobility discipline, protective gear, and calcification monitoring. Scientific research priority zone.

**Environment:** Vast regions where ancient forests have been mineralized — trees turned to stone, leaves preserved in crystal, entire ecosystems frozen mid-motion. The petrification process is ongoing; living organisms that remain stationary too long begin to calcify. Wind erosion has carved the stone trees into eerie formations.

**Flora:** Technically none — all original plant life is petrified. However, some organisms exploit the stone forest: lichen-equivalents that slowly digest minerite, parasitic vines that use petrified trees as supports while remaining carefully mobile, and fast-growing species that mature before calcification sets in.

**Fauna:** Small, quick, and never still. Everything that survives here has adapted to constant movement. Herbivores dart between feeding sites. Omnivores are rapid scavengers. Predators are burst-attack specialists — strike fast, move on. Maniacs are almost nonexistent; the calcification process seems to affect neurological tissue rapidly, and creatures that stop thinking clearly stop moving.

**Resources:** Preserved organic samples (scientific value), mineralized materials, Ancient artifacts often in pristine condition (petrification preserves everything), crystallized compounds unique to the calcification process. *(Rare specimens, research value)*

**Hazards:** Calcification (keep moving), sharp terrain, sudden predator attacks, getting trapped in narrow passages.

**Atmosphere:** Eerie, melancholic, suspended. Walking through the Petrified Expanse feels like moving through a photograph — everything caught in a moment that will never end. Some colonists find it beautiful. Others find it a warning.

---

#### 8. Frozen Reaches

> *Cold preserves. Cold kills.*

**Survival Tier: III (Hostile)** — Requires thermal insulation systems, high-calorie rations, and cold-weather survival training. Pristine Ancient sites under ice.

**Environment:** Polar and high-altitude regions where temperatures remain far below freezing. Ice sheets, glaciers, frozen lakes, and snow-covered terrain. Limited sunlight during winter cycles; during summer, the light is constant but provides little warmth. The air is thin in high-altitude zones.

**Flora:** Hardy extremophiles that survive by antifreeze compounds, thermal vents, or dormancy. Ice-algae colonies that photosynthesize through ice. Hibernating plants that complete their entire reproductive cycle during brief thaw periods. Subsurface root networks that tap geothermal warmth.

**Fauna:** Heavily insulated creatures with slow metabolisms. Herbivores migrate following thaw patterns or subsist on ice-algae. Omnivores cache food during abundance periods. Predators are marathon hunters — they outlast prey rather than outrun it. Maniacs are rare; most creatures here are too focused on energy conservation to waste it on pointless aggression.

**Resources:** Preserved organic specimens (frozen), pure water (ice), cold-stable compounds, Ancient installations (often perfectly preserved under ice), occasional temporal anomalies (time moves strangely in extreme cold). *(Pristine artifacts, preserved specimens)*

**Hazards:** Hypothermia, ice collapse, hidden crevasses, blizzards, slow-stalking predators, caloric demands (you burn energy fast).

**Atmosphere:** Silent, vast, indifferent. The Frozen Reaches don't feel hostile — they feel empty of feeling entirely. The cold doesn't hate you. It simply doesn't care if you exist.

---

#### 9. Scarred Badlands

> *What's left after everything is taken.*

**Survival Tier: I (Frontier)** — Manageable with standard equipment, water supplies, and situational awareness. Recommended starting biome for Helix-affiliated arrivals.

**Environment:** Arid, eroded terrain where some ancient catastrophe — geological or otherwise — stripped the land bare. Canyons, mesas, exposed rock strata, and mineral veins visible from the surface. Water is scarce. Shade is scarcer. The temperature swings between brutal heat during day and freezing cold at night.

**Flora:** Survival specialists. Deep-rooted plants that tap underground water. Cacti-equivalents that store moisture. Quick-blooming species that complete life cycles after rare rains. Parasitic organisms that steal water from other plants.

**Fauna:** Tough, territorial, and aggressive. Resources are scarce enough that every creature defends what it has. Herbivores compete fiercely for limited vegetation. Omnivores are heavily carnivorous — plant matter is too valuable to share. Predators are efficient; they cannot afford to miss. Maniacs may simply be desperately hungry creatures pushed past behavioral limits.

**Resources:** Exposed mineral deposits (easy extraction), fossil equivalents, water (rare but valuable), hardy biological samples, Ancient ruins (often prominent landmarks in flat terrain). *(Accessible minerals, easy extraction)*

**Hazards:** Dehydration, heat stroke, temperature swings, aggressive wildlife, unstable terrain (erosion), flash floods in canyons during rare storms.

**Atmosphere:** Harsh, stripped, honest. The Scarred Badlands have no secrets — everything is exposed. There's a brutal clarity here. You see exactly what you're dealing with, and it's all trying to kill you.

---

#### 10. Anomaly Zones

> *Reality is optional.*

**Survival Tier: IV (Extreme)** — Requires elite anomaly-resistant equipment, experienced teams, psychological screening, and corporate authorization. Highest risk, highest reward. Many expeditions do not return.

**Environment:** Regions where the Anomaly phenomenon has become permanent and dominant. Physics is unreliable. Geography shifts. Time stutters. The visual appearance varies wildly — some zones look almost normal until you notice the shadows pointing the wrong direction; others are immediately, obviously wrong, filled with impossible geometries and colors that shouldn't exist.

**Flora:** Mutated, warped, or replaced entirely by something else. Plants that grow in impossible directions. Organisms that exist partially out of phase with normal spacetime. Things that might be plants, or might be extensions of the Anomaly itself.

**Fauna:** Classification breaks down. Creatures here may have been normal organisms before exposure, or may have originated elsewhere entirely. Behavioral categories become unreliable — a "herbivore" that grazes on distorted space, a "predator" that hunts in temporal loops. Maniacs are common; exposure to Anomaly effects seems to damage whatever regulates aggression.

**Resources:** Anomaly-forged materials (unique and valuable), Ancient artifacts (Anomaly zones often overlap with ruins), exotic compounds, data about Anomaly physics (scientific value). The resources are exceptional. The extraction cost is measured in lives. *(Exceptional value, unique materials unavailable elsewhere)*

**Hazards:** Everything. Reality distortion, temporal displacement, spatial traps, corrupted wildlife, psychological effects, equipment failure, getting lost in ways that don't involve geography. Standard survival rules don't apply.

**Atmosphere:** Wrong. The only consistent quality of Anomaly Zones is the persistent feeling that you shouldn't be here. Not fear exactly — more fundamental, like the universe itself is objecting to your presence.

### The Ancients

The previous inhabitants of Terminus left no name for themselves. Colonists call them simply "The Ancients" or, in corporate documentation, "Prior Inhabitants" (PI).

**What we know:**
- They were technologically advanced — beyond current human capability, but not incomprehensibly so. They built with materials we can almost replicate. Their energy systems follow principles we can almost understand.
- Their architecture suggests beings roughly humanoid in scale, but the proportions are... wrong. Doorways too tall. Stairs with uneven spacing. Chairs with too many joints.
- They vanished. No bodies. No war damage. No signs of evacuation. Simply... gone.

**What we suspect:**
- They are not dead. Seismic sensors detect movement in sealed underground complexes. Energy signatures flicker in ruins that should be inert. Some expeditions into deep sites have reported auditory anomalies — sounds that *almost* form patterns.
- The Ancients may be dormant. Waiting. For what, no one knows.

Corporate policy officially classifies the Ancients as "extinct." Officially.

### Anomalies

Scattered across Terminus are zones where reality behaves incorrectly. Colonists call them Anomalies.

**Common types:**
- **Temporal Stutters**: Localized time distortions. Minutes pass in seconds, or hours stretch into days.
- **Spatial Tears**: Areas where geometry fails. Distances change. Directions reverse.
- **Echo Fields**: Zones where sounds, images, or even thoughts from the past replay on loop.
- **Null Pockets**: Places where technology fails, biological processes slow, and light dims for no detectable reason.

Anomalies are valuable — they often contain Ancient artifacts or rare materials formed by the distortions. They are also lethal. Expeditions into Anomaly zones have a 23% casualty rate.

The corporations deny any connection between Anomalies and the Ancients' disappearance. The corporations are lying.

### Flora and Fauna

Terminus hosts a complete biosphere, though one distinctly alien to Earth-derived life. Key characteristics:

- **Bioluminescence is common**: Many species generate light, creating forests that glow at night.
- **Silicon-carbon hybrid biology**: Some organisms incorporate silicon structures, making them partially mineral.
- **Aggressive adaptation**: Terminus life evolves fast — visibly fast. Species introduced to new environments change within generations.
- **Symbiotic complexity**: Most organisms exist in intricate symbiotic webs. Removing one species can collapse entire ecosystems.

Some creatures are dangerous. A few are nightmarish. The deep caves and Ancient ruins harbor things that the corporations have classified as "Priority Containment Entities" — and refuse to elaborate further.

### Creature Behavioral Classifications

Corporate xenobiologists have categorized Terminus fauna into four behavioral classes based on threat response patterns. Understanding these classifications is essential for survival.

#### Herbivores (Threat Level: Low)

Herbivores feed exclusively on plant matter and pose minimal threat under normal circumstances. They will flee from larger creatures and ignore smaller ones — unless cornered.

**Threat Response:** Defensive only. A herbivore attacks when:
- It perceives no escape route
- Its young are threatened
- It is injured and cannot flee

**Colonial Guidance:** Give them space. Most herbivore injuries come from panicked colonists blocking escape routes. A creature that could have run will fight if it must.

*Examples: Grazers, fungal browsers, canopy feeders*

#### Omnivores (Threat Level: Moderate)

Omnivores are opportunistic feeders that consume both plant and animal matter. Their behavior is driven by a calculation: is this target worth the energy and risk?

**Threat Response:** Predatory under specific conditions. An omnivore attacks when:
- It is significantly larger than the target AND
- It is experiencing hunger (not merely appetite)

Omnivores will generally ignore creatures of similar or larger size. They prefer easier meals.

**Colonial Guidance:** Size matters. Travel in groups when possible. Well-fed omnivores are largely harmless; starving ones are not. Seasonal food shortages correlate with increased omnivore aggression.

*Examples: Scavenger packs, adaptive foragers, opportunistic hunters*

#### Predators (Threat Level: High)

Predators are evolved killing machines optimized for hunting other creatures. However, unlike Earth carnivores, Terminus predators are remarkably efficient — they kill to eat, not for sport, territory, or dominance displays.

**Threat Response:** Calculated aggression. A predator attacks when:
- It is hungry and identifies viable prey, OR
- It perceives a threat to itself and cannot retreat

A satiated predator is often surprisingly docile. Some colonists have reported walking within meters of feeding predators without incident. (This is not recommended.)

**Colonial Guidance:** Know the signs of hunger — a hunting predator moves differently than a resting one. Predators avoid unnecessary risk; appearing dangerous is sometimes enough. Retreat slowly if possible. Running triggers pursuit instincts.

*Examples: Apex stalkers, pack hunters, ambush specialists*

#### Maniacs (Threat Level: Extreme)

The colonial term "maniac" applies to creatures that defy normal behavioral logic. These organisms attack anything they perceive, constantly, without apparent regard for self-preservation, energy expenditure, or tactical advantage.

**Threat Response:** Unrestricted aggression. A maniac attacks:
- Any creature it detects (visual, auditory, or chemical)
- Regardless of size differential
- Regardless of its own condition
- Including members of its own species (rare, but documented)

Xenobiologists theorize maniacs may be experiencing neurological corruption — possibly from Anomaly exposure, parasitic infection, or Ancient bio-contamination. Their behavior serves no observable evolutionary purpose.

**Colonial Guidance:** Do not engage unless necessary. Do not attempt to reason with behavior — there is no behavior, only violence. Maniacs cannot be intimidated, negotiated with, or waited out. If you encounter one: fight, flee, or hide. There are no other options.

*Examples: Void-touched fauna, corrupted specimens, deep-ruin dwellers*

---

**Field Note:** Behavioral classifications are guidelines, not guarantees. Individual creatures may deviate from expected patterns due to injury, disease, breeding cycles, or exposure to Anomalies. Treat all wildlife as potentially dangerous until confirmed otherwise.

## Part III: The Factions

Three megacorporations control Terminus. Each has its own territory, ideology, and methods. All three want the same thing: profit, power, and control of humanity's future. They simply disagree on how to achieve it.

### Verdant Dynamics

> *"Sustainability is Profitability"*

**Public Image:** The green corporation. Verdant Dynamics markets itself as the ethical choice — the company that will develop Terminus without destroying it. Their recruitment materials emphasize harmony with the ecosystem, long-term thinking, and responsible stewardship.

**Private Reality:** Verdant's environmentalism is a brand, not a belief. They discovered early that "sustainable" practices were cheaper in the long run, and that green marketing commanded premium prices back in the colonies. They exploit Terminus exactly as aggressively as their competitors — they're simply better at hiding it.

**Methods:**
- Bioengineering native species for industrial purposes rather than importing Earth-based alternatives
- "Selective harvesting" that technically preserves ecosystems while extracting maximum value
- Aggressive PR campaigns painting competitors as planet-killers
- Carbon credit trading schemes that exist primarily on paper

**Territory:** The temperate forest regions, coastal zones, and the massive fungal networks of the western continent.

**Aesthetic:** Organic curves, living architecture, green and brown color schemes. Their facilities look like they grew rather than were built. This is intentional misdirection.

**Internal Culture:** Performatively progressive. Employees are encouraged to participate in conservation theater while meeting aggressive extraction quotas. Cognitive dissonance is a job requirement.

---

### Helix Extraction

> *"Humanity's Survival Demands Sacrifice"*

**Public Image:** The pragmatists. Helix Extraction doesn't pretend to be gentle. They acknowledge that colonizing Terminus requires difficult choices, and they position themselves as the corporation brave enough to make them. Their messaging emphasizes duty, necessity, and the hard truths of survival.

**Private Reality:** Helix's leadership genuinely believes their own rhetoric — which makes them more dangerous, not less. They've convinced themselves that maximum extraction is a moral imperative. Every forest cleared, every species driven extinct, every worker sacrificed to unsafe conditions is justified by humanity's survival. This certainty permits atrocities.

**Methods:**
- Strip-mining operations that scar entire regions
- Aggressive chemical processing with minimal environmental controls
- "Productivity optimization" programs that push workers to dangerous limits
- Weaponized research into Ancient technology, regardless of risk

**Territory:** The mineral-rich mountain ranges, volcanic zones, and deep cavern systems of the eastern territories.

**Aesthetic:** Industrial brutalism. Exposed metal, warning stripes, machinery that prioritizes function over form. Their facilities look exactly like what they are.

**Internal Culture:** Militaristic hierarchy. Loyalty is rewarded. Questioning orders is not. Workers are provided with excellent hazard pay and terrible survival odds.

---

### Nexus Frontiers

> *"Connecting Worlds, Creating Opportunities"*

**Public Image:** The moderates. Nexus Frontiers positions itself as the reasonable middle ground — neither recklessly exploitative nor hypocritically "green." They emphasize trade, cooperation, and mutual benefit. Their marketing suggests they're the corporation that brings people together.

**Private Reality:** Nexus has no ideology beyond opportunity. They trade with everyone, spy on everyone, and position themselves to profit regardless of which faction comes out ahead. They've sold Verdant's research to Helix and Helix's extraction techniques to Verdant. Their "moderation" is simply strategic flexibility.

**Methods:**
- Operating the primary trading posts and transport networks between faction territories
- Intelligence gathering disguised as neutral commerce
- Playing factions against each other to create profitable instability
- "Consulting services" that ensure they have leverage over everyone

**Territory:** The river networks, coastal trade hubs, and the neutral zones between Verdant and Helix territories. They also control most of the orbital facilities.

**Aesthetic:** Clean, professional, adaptable. Their facilities are designed to feel welcoming to members of any faction. Lots of glass, neutral colors, and corporate-friendly design.

**Internal Culture:** Competitive meritocracy built on information asymmetry. Employees are rewarded for knowing things others don't. Everyone is watching everyone. Trust is a liability.

### Faction Leadership

#### Director Emmeline Voss — Verdant Dynamics

> *"We're not here to save this planet. We're here to prove that saving planets is good business."*

**Age:** 67 (with extensive life-extension treatments; appears mid-40s)

**Background:** Born on Cygnus Station, a failing orbital colony known for its recycling-dependent closed ecosystems. Voss grew up watching her community squeeze every calorie from every scrap, treating waste as sin and efficiency as religion. She earned degrees in ecological economics and corporate communications before joining Verdant's predecessor company as a junior marketing analyst.

Her rise was methodical. She identified that "green" branding commanded premium prices in resource-starved colonies, then built an entire corporate philosophy around that insight. She orchestrated Verdant's merger with three smaller ecological firms, rebranded the combined entity, and positioned herself as the visionary founder of a movement — despite having invented nothing but the packaging.

**Public Persona:** The Steward. Voss presents herself as a reluctant corporate leader who'd rather be planting trees — a scientist-philosopher forced into the boardroom by necessity. Her speeches emphasize long-term thinking, intergenerational responsibility, and the poetry of balanced ecosystems. She wears undyed natural fabrics, keeps native Terminus plants in her office, and has been photographed releasing rehabilitated wildlife into protected preserves.

Colonists call her "Mother Green." The nickname was focus-grouped before being "organically" adopted.

**Private Reality:** Voss has never experienced a genuine moment of environmental sentiment in her life. She views ecosystems as machines and species as components — valuable when useful, discardable when not. Her "sustainable harvesting" quotas are calculated to extract the maximum possible resources while maintaining the *appearance* of conservation. When a protected species interferes with profitable operations, it quietly becomes "invasive" and is scheduled for "management."

What Voss *does* believe in is control. She grew up in a world where survival depended on perfect resource management, and she's determined to manage Terminus the same way — with herself at the center.

**Leadership Style:** Soft power. Voss never gives direct orders when suggestions will suffice, never threatens when implications will do. She cultivates personal loyalty through mentorship, remembers the names of employees' children, and sends handwritten notes on promotions. Those who please her rise. Those who disappoint her find their careers quietly suffocating.

She is patient, strategic, and utterly without mercy.

**Relationships:**
- **Marcus Thorne (Helix):** Contempt masked as pity. Voss sees Thorne as a useful idiot — his aggression makes Verdant look reasonable by comparison. She's leaked information to him twice, knowing his predictable overreaction would damage Helix's reputation and strengthen her own.
- **Yuki Tanaka (Nexus):** Wary respect. Voss knows Tanaka is the most dangerous person on Terminus precisely because she's impossible to predict. They've worked together when convenient, betrayed each other when profitable, and neither pretends to trust the other.
- **The ICC:** Voss maintains impeccable relations with the Council. She serves on three advisory committees, hosts visiting inspectors personally, and has never once received a regulatory citation. The ICC considers her a model corporate citizen. This is intentional.

**Dark Secret:** Twenty years ago, on Cygnus Station, a sealed environmental report predicted the colony's ecosystem would collapse within a decade. Young Emmeline Voss accessed that report, calculated that the collapse would create opportunities for whoever controlled emergency supplies, and suppressed it. Cygnus Station fell two years ahead of schedule. Forty thousand people died, including her parents and younger brother.

Voss had already transferred to corporate headquarters. She used the tragedy to launch her career, positioning herself as a grieving survivor dedicated to preventing future environmental catastrophes.

She has never told anyone. She has also never stopped having the dream where her brother asks why she didn't warn them.

**What She Truly Wants:** Control over Terminus's biosphere — complete, total, permanent. Not to preserve it, but to *own* it. Every organism catalogued, every ecosystem mapped, every variable accounted for. A world that runs exactly as she designs it to run, forever.

The Ancients' technology might make that possible. Voss has a dedicated research team studying their environmental manipulation systems. The results are classified above board level.

#### Administrator Marcus Thorne — Helix Extraction

> *"Every resource we don't extract is a child who dies on a failing station. Every species we don't study is knowledge that could save millions. Tell me again how restraint is moral."*

**Age:** 58 (minimal life-extension; he considers it "soft")

**Background:** Born in the deep mining warrens of Proxima Station, where children started work shifts at twelve and most adults didn't see fifty. Thorne's father died in a tunnel collapse when Marcus was fifteen. His mother worked herself to death covering the family's debt by the time he was twenty. He inherited nothing but a contract obligation and a bone-deep understanding that survival costs lives.

Thorne clawed his way up through Helix's mining operations — foreman, site manager, regional director, executive. Every promotion was earned in blood, mostly other people's. He was never cruel for cruelty's sake, but he never let sentiment override necessity. When a mine needed to meet quota, the mine met quota. When workers needed to be pushed harder, they were pushed harder. When someone had to make the call that would get people killed, Thorne made the call.

He's been making those calls for forty years. He's made peace with it.

**Public Persona:** The Necessary Man. Thorne presents himself as the hard-faced realist willing to do what others won't. His speeches acknowledge that Helix's methods are brutal, then argue that brutality is honesty — that the other corporations exploit just as aggressively while lying about it. He wears his company's industrial aesthetic: practical clothing, visible tool marks on his hands, no pretense of polish.

Workers either hate him or worship him. There's rarely middle ground.

**Private Reality:** Thorne genuinely believes every word he says. That's what makes him terrifying.

He has constructed an elaborate moral framework in which maximum extraction is humanity's sacred duty. Every tree felled is lumber for colonial ships. Every mineral mined is fuel for survival. Every worker who dies in the process is a soldier in humanity's war against extinction. The math is simple: a few thousand casualties now versus billions dead when the colonies finally fail.

The fact that this framework justifies whatever Thorne wants to do anyway is, he would argue, beside the point.

**Leadership Style:** Direct command. Thorne gives orders, expects them followed, and punishes failure visibly. He promotes from within, rewards productivity above all else, and maintains a personal presence at major operations — he's been known to spend weeks on mining platforms, working shifts alongside employees.

This isn't theater. Thorne genuinely believes leaders should share risks with workers. It's the only form of respect he understands.

**Relationships:**
- **Emmeline Voss (Verdant):** Seething hatred. Thorne considers Voss the worst kind of hypocrite — someone who exploits exactly as he does while pretending moral superiority. He's launched three major sabotage operations against Verdant, each designed to expose their "green" practices as fraud. All three failed. He suspects Voss knew they were coming.
- **Yuki Tanaka (Nexus):** Grudging necessity. Thorne despises Tanaka's slipperiness but needs Nexus's trade networks. He's aware she's selling information to his enemies. He's also aware he has no alternative. This rankles him constantly.
- **The ICC:** Thorne views the Council as bureaucratic parasites who've never worked an honest shift. He complies with regulations minimally, fights every citation, and has been heard calling the Council "humanity's suicide note" in private meetings.

**Dark Secret:** Thorne has a son.

Viktor Thorne was born thirty-two years ago to a woman Marcus barely remembers from a shore leave on Titan Station. Marcus learned about the boy's existence when Viktor was already eight. He sent money. He never visited.

Viktor is now a doctor working in a Verdant medical facility, treating patients injured by Helix operations. He goes by his mother's surname. He has never acknowledged his father. Marcus has a photograph of Viktor — taken without his knowledge by a Helix intelligence operative — in a locked drawer in his private quarters.

He's never told anyone about Viktor. He tells himself it's to protect the boy from being used as leverage. The truth is simpler: Marcus Thorne, who has never flinched from any necessary sacrifice, cannot bear to face what his son thinks of him.

**What He Truly Wants:** To be proven right. To live long enough to see Terminus supply the resources that save humanity's remaining colonies. To stand in front of the ICC, in front of Voss, in front of everyone who called him a monster, and show them the survival statistics and ask: *was I wrong?*

The Ancients' ruins contain technology that could revolutionize resource extraction. Thorne has authorized research into those systems at any cost. Some of his deep-site teams have stopped reporting in. He's sent more.

#### Coordinator Yuki Tanaka — Nexus Frontiers

> *"Information wants to be free. I'm just helping it along — for a reasonable fee."*

**Age:** 52 (age ambiguous due to extensive but subtle modifications)

**Background:** Yuki Tanaka's early history is a matter of some debate, primarily because she's seeded at least six contradictory versions into various databases. What can be verified: she emerged in Nexus's intelligence division in her mid-twenties, already possessing skills and knowledge that suggested either elite training or extensive field experience. Within a decade, she controlled the division. Within two decades, she controlled Nexus.

Her rise involved no violence, no scandals, and no visible conflict. Rivals simply found themselves reassigned to undesirable postings. Critics discovered their private communications leaked at unfortunate moments. Allies were rewarded with information that made their careers. By the time anyone realized Tanaka was consolidating power, the consolidation was complete.

She has never explained where she came from. The most popular theories involve colonial intelligence agencies, but there are darker rumors. Someone with her skills doesn't emerge from nowhere.

**Public Persona:** The Reasonable Broker. Tanaka presents herself as Terminus's diplomat — the calm voice that keeps the other factions from open war. Her speeches emphasize cooperation, mutual benefit, and the dangers of extremism in any direction. She dresses professionally but not memorably, speaks clearly but not quotably, and projects an aura of competent moderation.

Most people who meet her once forget her face within a week. This is deliberate.

**Private Reality:** Tanaka has no loyalty to Nexus, no attachment to Terminus, and no stake in humanity's survival beyond her own. She is a creature of pure information — she collects it, trades it, weaponizes it, and finds the process endlessly fascinating. Everyone else on Terminus is playing to win. Tanaka is playing because the game is interesting.

This makes her unpredictable in ways the other leaders cannot model. Voss and Thorne operate on comprehensible motivations: power, ideology, profit. Tanaka will sabotage a profitable deal because it's become boring. She'll protect an enemy because their destruction would simplify the board too much. She'll share information freely with a rival just to see what they do with it.

She is, by a significant margin, the most dangerous person on the planet.

**Leadership Style:** Decentralized orchestration. Tanaka rarely gives direct orders. Instead, she ensures her subordinates have access to information that makes certain choices obvious. Nexus employees feel like they're acting independently; in reality, their decisions are shaped by carefully curated data flows. Those who figure out what's happening are promoted. Those who don't remain useful.

She meets with key personnel individually, in informal settings, and remembers everything anyone has ever told her. This is not politeness. It's intelligence gathering.

**Relationships:**
- **Emmeline Voss (Verdant):** Intellectual equals who enjoy their rivalry. Tanaka finds Voss's long-term planning elegant but predictable. She's disrupted three of Voss's major initiatives not for profit but because she wanted to see if Voss could adapt. (She could. Tanaka was pleased.)
- **Marcus Thorne (Helix):** A useful tool. Tanaka finds Thorne's directness refreshing but his ideology tiresome. She's manipulated him into conflicts with Verdant at least twice, each time selling the intelligence to both sides.
- **The ICC:** Tanaka's relationship with the Council is complex. She provides them with carefully selected information that makes them feel informed while obscuring matters she prefers they not notice. Three Council members believe she's their personal asset. She's reported on all three to their rivals.

**Dark Secret:** Yuki Tanaka died thirty-one years ago.

The woman operating under that name is... something else. During an expedition into an Ancient ruin in her early career, something happened. The records are sealed, the other team members are dead, and Tanaka emerged different. Not inhuman — she bleeds, ages (slowly), and has normal biological readings. But those who knew her before the incident insist she's not the same person.

She has memories of being Yuki Tanaka. She has Yuki Tanaka's skills and knowledge. She does not have Yuki Tanaka's fingerprints, and her DNA contains markers that don't exist in any human database.

Tanaka has never told anyone. She's not certain what she is, and the uncertainty is the one thing in the universe she cannot tolerate. She's been searching the Ancient ruins for answers ever since. The planet-machine theory developed by the disappeared Nexus analyst? Tanaka didn't have her killed. She has her hidden, alive, still working on the problem.

**What She Truly Wants:** To understand what she became. To find whatever part of the Ancients touched her and comprehend it. If that requires destabilizing all three factions, awakening dormant alien intelligences, or triggering the planetary machine — she'll consider those acceptable research costs.

She's patient. She's been patient for thirty-one years. But the dreams are getting clearer, and she's beginning to hear patterns in the static between the stars.

### The Triumvirate Dynamic

The three leaders meet quarterly at formal summits, monthly via secure communication, and constantly through their intelligence services. Their interactions follow predictable patterns:

**Voss and Thorne:** Open antagonism. They despise each other and barely pretend otherwise. Their conflicts are ideological (at least on Thorne's side) and provide useful theater for both factions. Colonists see the green/industry divide and choose sides; neither leader discourages this, as tribal loyalty is useful.

**Voss and Tanaka:** Careful alliance. They've cooperated against Helix multiple times, though both know the partnership is temporary. Voss considers Tanaka a useful instrument; Tanaka considers Voss a fascinating study in self-deception. They occasionally share wine and discuss philosophy. Neither trusts the other to pass the salt.

**Thorne and Tanaka:** Tense necessity. Thorne needs Nexus's networks and hates needing them. Tanaka finds Thorne's transparency almost endearing. She's protected him from two assassination attempts (one from Verdant, one from his own board) without telling him, because a predictable Helix is easier to manage than a chaotic succession crisis.

**All Three:** None of them knows about the others' deepest secrets. Voss's role in the Cygnus collapse, Thorne's son, Tanaka's transformation — these are vulnerabilities each guards absolutely. If any of this information surfaced, the balance of power would shift catastrophically.

Someone, somewhere, is collecting these secrets. The three leaders don't know who. They don't know why. They're all looking.

### Faction Headquarters

Each corporation maintains a primary hub in their territory — the administrative, economic, and cultural heart of their operation on Terminus.

#### The Verdant Dynamics Hub: **Canopy**

> *"Where growth is guided."*

**Location:** Deep within the temperate rainforest of the western continent, built into and around a grove of massive fungal trees that reach over two hundred meters.

**Design Philosophy:** Living architecture. Canopy isn't built — it's grown. Structures are cultivated from engineered plant matter, shaped over months into buildings that breathe, filter air, and produce oxygen. Walkways spiral up through the canopy layer, connecting platforms that hang between trunks. Bioluminescent vines provide lighting. Water flows through living channels carved into bark.

From a distance, Canopy is almost invisible — just another part of the forest. This is intentional.

**Atmosphere:** Serene, deliberate, controlled. Natural sounds are amplified through acoustic engineering — birdsong equivalents, rustling leaves, flowing water. The air smells of growth and decay in balance. Temperatures are regulated by the living architecture itself. It feels like paradise.

It is also a constant, subtle reminder that Verdant controls nature here. Nothing grows without permission.

**Key Features:**
- **The Atrium**: Central administrative hub grown from a single modified organism; Director Voss's office occupies the highest chamber
- **The Nurseries**: Research facilities where native species are "optimized" for commercial applications
- **Communion Halls**: Cafeterias and social spaces where employees are encouraged to "connect with the ecosystem" (mandatory attendance tracking applies)
- **The Roots**: Underground processing facilities where the actual extraction work happens, carefully hidden from surface-level aesthetics

**First Impression for New Arrivals:** Wonder. Canopy is beautiful, welcoming, and profoundly impressive. Only after weeks do recruits notice the control — the way paths guide movement, the way "natural" spaces are precisely managed, the way even the wildlife seems to perform on cue.

---

#### The Helix Extraction Hub: **Ironhold**

> *"Strength through industry."*

**Location:** Built into and around a volcanic mountain range on the eastern continent, directly atop some of the richest mineral deposits on Terminus.

**Design Philosophy:** Functional brutalism. Ironhold makes no pretense of beauty. It is a machine for extracting resources, housing workers, and projecting power. Structures are prefabricated metal and reinforced concrete, bolted together with visible rivets, marked with safety warnings and operational classifications. Steam vents from geothermal taps. Ore conveyors run overhead. The sound of heavy machinery never stops.

The message is clear: this is where work happens.

**Atmosphere:** Loud, hot, purposeful. The air tastes of metal and carries trace industrial particulates (within "acceptable" limits). Shift changes create rivers of workers in practical coveralls. Everything is labeled, scheduled, optimized. There is no wasted space, no decorative element, no concession to comfort beyond what's necessary for productivity.

It feels like the inside of an engine. Some find this oppressive. Others find it honest.

**Key Features:**
- **The Forge**: Central command complex where Administrator Thorne maintains his office — spartanly furnished, with real-time production displays covering every wall
- **The Warrens**: Worker housing carved into the mountainside; cramped but functional, with excellent hazard pay rates posted at every entrance
- **The Crucible**: Training facilities where new recruits learn extraction techniques, equipment operation, and survival protocols
- **Deep Shaft Seven**: The primary mining operation, descending kilometers into the planet's crust; restricted areas increase with depth

**First Impression for New Arrivals:** Intimidation. Ironhold does not welcome — it evaluates. New recruits are immediately sorted, assessed, and assigned. The orientation speech makes clear: produce or leave. There is no third option.

But for those who stay: Helix takes care of its own. The hazard pay is real. The medical facilities are excellent. And there's a brutal camaraderie among workers who've survived the same shifts.

---

#### The Nexus Frontiers Hub: **Meridian**

> *"The center of everything."*

**Location:** A coastal city at the confluence of three major rivers, positioned precisely at the border where Verdant and Helix territories meet — and controlling access to both.

**Design Philosophy:** Adaptive professionalism. Meridian is designed to make everyone comfortable and no one at home. The architecture is modular, reconfigurable, and deliberately neutral — clean lines, glass facades, temperature-controlled interiors. It could be a corporate campus on any world in any era. This blandness is strategic: Meridian belongs to no culture, which means any culture can conduct business here.

**Atmosphere:** Busy, transactional, anonymous. Meridian runs on commerce. Traders from all three factions move through markets and exchanges. Information brokers operate from nondescript offices. Deals are struck in cafes designed for privacy. Everyone is polite. No one trusts anyone.

The city never sleeps — there's always a shift change somewhere, always a market opening, always a meeting running late.

**Key Features:**
- **The Exchange**: Terminus's primary trading hub, where resources, contracts, and information change hands; Coordinator Tanaka maintains a modest office here, indistinguishable from dozens of others (her actual command center's location is unknown)
- **The Commons**: Neutral social spaces where faction members can interact without corporate oversight — theoretically
- **The Archive**: Nexus's data storage facilities, housing records on every transaction, every communication, every individual who's ever passed through; access is tiered, and the deepest levels don't officially exist
- **Port Meridian**: The spaceport handling transport to orbital facilities and, eventually, back to the colonies

**First Impression for New Arrivals:** Opportunity. Meridian feels like a place where anyone can make something of themselves. The city is clean, efficient, and full of possibilities. Nexus recruitment emphasizes flexibility, meritocracy, and the chance to write your own story.

What takes longer to notice: everyone is watching. Every transaction is logged. Every relationship is mapped. In Meridian, information is currency — and Nexus holds all accounts.

Each faction maintains a dedicated processing area for new arrivals — the first experience recruits have on Terminus.

**Verdant — The Threshold**
A carefully designed "natural" clearing where dropships land on living platforms. New arrivals are guided through a "purification walk" — a winding forest path with air filtration integrated into the foliage. The message: you're entering something precious. Behave accordingly.

**Nexus — Welcome Center Alpha**
A comfortable transit lounge with complimentary refreshments, personal greeting attendants, and orientation materials available in forty languages. Everything is pleasant. Everything is recorded. The message: we're here to help. (Terms and conditions apply.)

## Part IV: The Conflict

### Why Not Open War?

The three corporations could fight directly. They have the resources, the personnel, and certainly the motivation. But they don't, for three interconnected reasons:

**1. The ICC Is Watching**

The Intercolonial Corporate Council funded this expedition. They retain nominal authority over all three factions and have the power to revoke territorial charters. More importantly, the colonies are watching. Shareholders are watching. The narrative of "humanity united in hope" is worth trillions in investment and political capital.

Open warfare would shatter that narrative. The ICC would intervene. Contracts would be voided. Executives would face tribunals.

No one can afford that.

**2. Mutual Assured Destruction**

Each corporation has enough military capability to devastate the others — but not enough to win cleanly. Open war would leave all three factions crippled, their infrastructure destroyed, and the remaining colonists caught in the crossfire.

Terminus needs a million living workers, not a battlefield of corpses.

**3. Plausible Deniability Is Cheaper**

Sabotage, espionage, and "accidents" accomplish most strategic goals at a fraction of the cost — in resources and reputation — of open conflict. Why deploy an army when a single operative can contaminate a water supply, corrupt a database, or arrange a tragic equipment failure?

The corporations wage constant shadow war while publicly attending the same galas and signing the same cooperation agreements.

### The Shadow War

Conflict on Terminus operates on two levels:

**Corporate Espionage**
- Data theft and counter-intelligence operations
- Sabotage of research projects and extraction operations
- Recruitment (or elimination) of key personnel
- Economic manipulation through trade networks

This is subtle, deniable, and constant. Every corporation maintains intelligence divisions that officially don't exist.

**Frontier Skirmishes**
- "Bandit" attacks on remote facilities (conducted by off-book mercenary units)
- "Equipment malfunctions" that strand expeditions in dangerous territory
- "Wildlife incidents" involving creatures that were definitely not weaponized
- Territorial disputes in unassigned regions, settled by whoever's security team arrives first

This is louder, but still deniable. When Helix miners are attacked by "unknown hostiles" in disputed territory, everyone knows Verdant was responsible. But without proof, the ICC can't act — and Helix's retaliation will be equally unprovable.

Workers on all sides have learned to watch their backs. The planet is dangerous enough without wondering if the person next to you is a saboteur.

## Part V: Life on Terminus

### New Arrivals — The Player Experience

> *"You survived the journey. That's the easy part."*
> — Standard orientation briefing, all factions

You are a new arrival on Terminus — one of thousands who step off the transport ships each month, contract chip embedded in your wrist, everything you own in a single pack.

**Who You Were**

Your past is your own. New arrivals come from every surviving colony, every background, every circumstance:

- **The Desperate**: Refugees from failing stations who signed contracts without reading them. Anything is better than slow suffocation.
- **The Ambitious**: Corporate climbers who see Terminus as the ultimate opportunity. New world, new rules, new fortunes to be made.
- **The Sentenced**: Criminals offered a choice — prison on a dying colony or labor on a living one. The contract is their parole.
- **The Believers**: Idealists who genuinely want to build something better for humanity. They tend to lose that idealism quickly.
- **The Runners**: People fleeing something — debts, enemies, memories. Terminus is far enough that the past might not follow.
- **The Curious**: Scientists, explorers, and dreamers drawn by the unknown. The planet calls to certain minds.

Whatever brought you here, it doesn't matter now. On Terminus, you are what you do.

**The Contract**

Every new arrival is bound to a faction through an employment contract. These contracts are:

- **Technically voluntary**: You chose to sign. The alternative was staying behind.
- **Long-term**: Standard terms are ten years. Early termination is expensive.
- **Comprehensive**: Your faction provides housing, food, equipment, and medical care. In exchange, your labor belongs to them.
- **Transferable**: You can buy out your contract and join a different faction — if you can afford it. Most can't. Some find other ways.

Contract-breakers become Unaffiliated — free, but without faction protection. The wilderness doesn't check your employment status.

**Your First Day**

Regardless of faction, every new arrival faces the same reality: you know nothing.

The training you received on the transport ship was generic, outdated, and insufficient. The orientation materials were marketing. The other new arrivals are as lost as you are. And the planet is trying to kill you in ways no one mentioned.

Survival requires learning:
- Which plants are edible (few) and which are lethal (many)
- Which creatures can be avoided and which must be fought
- Which ruins are worth exploring and which are sealed for good reason
- Which coworkers can be trusted and which report to intelligence divisions

Experience is the only teacher that matters. Those who learn survive. Those who don't become statistics — and cautionary tales for the next batch of arrivals.

**What You Can Become**

New arrivals start with nothing but potential. Through work, exploration, and survival, you can become:

- **An Explorer**: Mapping unknown territories, discovering new species, locating resources before anyone else
- **A Builder**: Constructing outposts, farms, factories — the infrastructure that turns wilderness into civilization
- **A Crafter**: Processing materials into equipment, supplies, and trade goods
- **A Researcher**: Studying Ancient technology, developing new applications, unlocking secrets
- **A Fighter**: Defending operations from wildlife, Anomalies, and faction rivals
- **A Trader**: Moving goods between territories, finding arbitrage opportunities, building networks
- **An Operative**: Working the shadow war — sabotage, espionage, and deniable operations

Most arrivals become some combination of these. Specialization is a luxury for those who survive long enough to develop it.

**The Question**

Every new arrival eventually faces the same question:

*What are you willing to do to survive?*

The answer defines everything that follows.

---

### The Colonists

One million people made the journey. They came from all six surviving colonies, from every level of society, united only by the willingness to leave everything behind for a chance at something better.

**Motivations vary:**
- True believers seeking humanity's renewal
- Desperate refugees from dying stations
- Corporate loyalists climbing the ladder
- Criminals offered pardons in exchange for service
- Scientists chasing discoveries impossible anywhere else
- Families hoping their children will inherit a living world instead of a dying one

Most colonists are contracted to one of the three corporations, their labor exchanged for housing, food, and the promise of eventual land ownership. These contracts are technically voluntary. The alternatives were technically worse.

### The Unaffiliated

Not everyone serves the corporations directly. A growing population of independents operates in the margins:

- **Freelance explorers** selling discoveries to the highest bidder
- **Traders** running goods between faction territories
- **Settlers** carving out homesteads in unclaimed wilderness
- **Salvagers** braving Ancient ruins for artifacts
- **Outlaws** who've broken their contracts and survive however they can

The corporations tolerate independents when useful and eliminate them when convenient. It's a precarious existence — but for some, it's the closest thing to freedom Terminus offers.

### The Work

Survival on Terminus requires constant labor:

- **Exploration**: Mapping unknown territories, cataloging species, locating resources
- **Extraction**: Mining, harvesting, processing raw materials
- **Construction**: Building settlements, infrastructure, defenses
- **Research**: Studying Ancient technology, developing new applications
- **Defense**: Protecting operations from wildlife, Anomalies, and "unknown hostiles"

Experience comes from doing. Those who survive long enough become invaluable — they know which plants are poisonous, which ruins are safe, which shortcuts don't lead to Anomalies. Knowledge is currency on Terminus.

## Part VI: Mysteries and Hooks

### The Silence of Earth

What happened to humanity's homeworld? Every colony has theories. Some believe it was environmental collapse. Others whisper about AI rebellion, plague, or civil war. A few fringe researchers note the timing — Earth went silent approximately when the Ancients would have gone dormant, if certain dating methods are correct.

Coincidence? Or connection?

### The Dreaming Beneath

Colonists near Ancient sites report shared dreams — visions of alien geometries, sounds that aren't sounds, a sense of something vast and patient becoming aware. The corporations suppress these reports. The phenomenon is spreading.

### The Second Moon's Secret

Whisper, the smaller moon, follows an orbit that should be impossible — it accelerates, decelerates, and occasionally seems to pause without any gravitational explanation. Long-range scans show structures on its surface. The corporations have agreed, for once, that no one is allowed to investigate.

What are they afraid of finding?

### The Deep Anomaly

Somewhere beneath the northern continent, seismographs detect a void — an empty space large enough to hold a city, too regular to be natural. The readings suggest movement inside. Something is alive down there, and it's big.

No expedition has reached it and returned.

### Pattern Recognition

A data analyst working for Nexus noticed something before her disappearance: the locations of Anomalies, Ancient ruins, and certain mineral deposits, when mapped in three dimensions, form a pattern. A structure. Perhaps a machine.

A machine the size of a planet.

Her files were classified. Her name was erased from corporate records. But copies exist, somewhere, in the hands of people asking dangerous questions.

## Part VII: Exo-Suit Systems

### Emergency Lockdown Mode

When an exo-suit's integrity drops to critical levels, the suit's AI activates **Emergency Lockdown Mode** — a protective state that immobilizes all suit functions to preserve pilot life support. The pilot remains conscious but cannot move or act until the suit is restored.

This system was developed after early colonial expeditions suffered catastrophic losses when wounded operatives continued pushing their damaged suits until total system failure. The lockdown protocol sacrifices mobility to preserve the pilot — a calculation the corporations consider acceptable, given the expense of training replacements.

Two recovery options exist for operatives in lockdown:

#### Emergency Rescue Protocol (ERP)

All faction-affiliated personnel can broadcast an S.O.S. signal triggering automated extraction to their faction hub:

- **Verdant Dynamics**: Bio-organic retrieval drones descend from Canopy's canopy layer, cocooning the operative in stabilizing gel before transport. The process is gentle. The cleanup afterward is not.

- **Helix Extraction**: Armored recovery crawlers emerge from the nearest Ironhold access tunnel, securing the operative in a pressurized shell before rapid transit through the subterranean network. Helix prides itself on reaching any operative within their territory in under fifteen minutes. They do not discuss the operatives they cannot reach in time.

- **Nexus Frontiers**: Rapid-response aerial units from Meridian provide extraction anywhere within Nexus-controlled territory. The flight is comfortable. The debrief afterward covers what intelligence was compromised during the operative's incapacitation.

Unaffiliated operatives can access ERP through Nexus emergency contracts — for a fee.

#### Emergency Reboot Kits

Field-deployable system restoration tools that bypass lockdown protocols and restore suit function on-site. Unlike standard repair kits (which restore suit integrity while the suit remains operational), reboot kits contain specialized firmware reset components and emergency power cells designed to override the lockdown state.

Higher-quality kits restore more suit integrity upon reboot. The best kits — particularly those incorporating Prior Inhabitant technology — can restore a suit to full operational capacity, though such items are rare and valuable.

Standard Suit Repair Kits cannot bypass Emergency Lockdown Mode. They are designed for field maintenance of functioning suits, not system recovery from critical failure.

### Tier I -- Frontier Biomes

#### Scarred Badlands (`void_plains`)

##### Creatures

**Void Crawler** -- Omnivore
> *"Skitters. Always skitters. Never walks."*
Small, multi-legged scavenger of the scarred plains. Void Crawlers are opportunistic feeders that avoid anything larger than themselves -- until hunger overrides caution. Their chitin yields a dull, void-saturated material useful for basic insulation. Individually trivial. In numbers, a different calculation entirely.

**Plains Grazer** -- Herbivore
> *"The closest thing to livestock this planet offers. Tastes terrible."*
Slow-moving herd animal that feeds on void-adapted grasses. Docile and easily startled, Plains Grazers are among the first creatures new arrivals learn to recognize. Their predictability makes them a reliable food source. Helix operatives consider them a waste of ammunition.

**Void Prowler** -- Predator
> *"Lean. Quick. Patient. Everything the Badlands teaches you to be."*
Ambush predator that uses rocky outcrops for concealment before striking with explosive acceleration. Void Prowlers hunt solo, targeting isolated prey. Experienced operatives watch for disturbed dust trails -- the only warning before a charge.

**Coastal Scuttler** -- Herbivore
> *"They eat rocks. Or the things growing on rocks. Hard to tell the difference."*
Small crustacean-like creature that feeds on exposed mineral deposits along the Badlands' edges. Docile unless stepped on, at which point they demonstrate that those mandibles are not purely decorative.

##### Flora

**Drought Cactus**
Water-storing succulent with tap roots reaching deep underground aquifers. The most reliable water source in the Badlands, if you can get through the spines. Verdant operatives have cultivated varieties that yield faster, though the wild specimens remain hardier.

**Void Tree**
Towering crimson tree adapted to the open plains. Its deep roots access water sources other plants cannot reach. Provides rare shade in the Badlands -- contested shade, usually.

**Void Fern**
Hardy fern producing small, nutrient-rich spores. Found across the Badlands and into the Luminous Canopy's edges. A staple foraging target for new arrivals. The rare variant shimmers with concentrated void energy.

##### Minerals

**Void Crystal**
Dark crystalline formation found in exposed strata throughout the Badlands. Easy to spot, easy to extract. The entry-level mining target for every prospector on Terminus. Rare specimens pulse with visible dark energy -- considerably more valuable.

**Void Slate**
Flat, layered stone saturated with void energy. Breaks into thin sheets useful for construction and basic crafting. Not glamorous. Reliably profitable.

##### Artifacts

**Badlands Beacon**
> *"Still transmitting. To what?"*
A corroded metal spire half-buried in void dust, still emitting a faint directional pulse despite millennia of exposure. The signal points toward the northern continent. Nobody has followed it far enough to find out why.

#### Luminous Canopy (`fungal_forest`)

##### Creatures

**Canopy Grazer** -- Herbivore
> *"Docile. Large. Do not corner."*
Massive herbivore feeding on fungal-tree canopy material. The Luminous Canopy's most prominent animal, visible from considerable distance by the bioluminescent residue on its hide. Verdant Dynamics claims a breeding program exists. The Grazers appear unaware of this arrangement.

**Spore Carrier** -- Omnivore
> *"The forest's immune system."*
Fungal creature that spreads defensive spores when disturbed. An opportunistic feeder serving as the Canopy's primary decomposer and -- inadvertently -- its most effective deterrent against casual trespass. The spores cause respiratory distress in unfiltered humans.

**Mycelial Stalker** -- Predator
> *"You don't hear it. You smell it -- when it's too late."*
Silent hunter moving through fungal undergrowth using chemical lures derived from the forest's own pheromone network. The Canopy's apex ambush predator. Verdant operatives have learned to read the subtle dimming of bioluminescence that signals a Stalker's chemical manipulation.

**Spore Beetle** -- Herbivore
> *"Armored. Slow. Releases a cloud when threatened that you do not want to breathe."*
Feeds on fungal fruiting bodies, processing them into the defensive spore clouds that give it its name. The beetles are individually harmless. Their chemical defense, however, can incapacitate an unprotected operative for hours.

##### Flora

**Luminous Vine**
Glowing vine network pulsing with soft bioluminescence -- the Canopy's signature feature. The light serves as both a nutrient transport mechanism and a warning system; damaged vines darken, signaling distress to the connected network. Rare specimens glow with intensity visible from orbit.

**Tendril Tree**
Towering fungal tree with grasping bioluminescent tendrils. Its purple bark pulses with slow rhythms that Verdant researchers insist is not communication. The trees form the structural backbone of the Canopy biome.

**Rare Bioluminescent Fungi** *(Rare)*
Exceptionally bright fungal clusters with concentrated biogenic compounds. Highly sought by pharmaceutical researchers and Verdant's bio-engineers.

**Ancient Spore Cluster** *(Epic)*
Primordial fungal formation predating colonial settlement. The spores contain unknown genetic sequences that suggest the Canopy's ecosystem is considerably older -- and more deliberate -- than initial surveys indicated.

##### Minerals

**Mycelial Cluster**
Dense fungal fiber formation with pharmaceutical and industrial applications. The organic-mineral composite properties make it uniquely valuable for bio-engineering. Rare specimens are shot through with luminescent fungal threads that suggest deeper mycorrhizal connections.

**Fungite Ore**
Mineral deposit heavily infiltrated by fungal mycelium. The resulting hybrid material has structural properties that neither component possesses alone. Verdant Dynamics holds seven patents on applications. They hold twelve more they haven't disclosed.

##### Artifacts

**Luminous Seed** *(Rare)*
> *"A perfectly spherical seed encased in crystallized sap, glowing with a steady bioluminescence that hasn't faded in the estimated centuries since formation."*
The seed is alive. It has been alive for longer than any organism on Terminus should be. What it's waiting to grow into is a question Verdant's xenobiologists prefer not to answer publicly.

#### Coastal Shallows (`tidal_pools`)

##### Creatures

**Tide Crab** -- Herbivore
Small crustacean scuttling along tidal flats. The most abundant creature in the Coastal Shallows and the baseline against which all other Terminus fauna are measured. Docile, crunchy, technically edible.

**Coastal Urchin** -- Herbivore
Spiny filter feeder anchored to rocks. Harvests detritus from water and occasionally from incautious fingers.

**Reef Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Opportunistic omnivore that picks through tidal debris. Will attack if hungry, which in the competitive Shallows means most of the time.

**Tidal Snapper** -- Predator
> *"Lightning-fast. From the deeper pools. Check before you wade."*
Aggressive crustacean lurking in deeper tidal pools. Its strike is faster than most operatives' reaction time. Nexus field guides recommend probing pools with a stick before stepping in. The Snapper population considers this a delivery service.

##### Flora

**Tidal Kelp**
Common kelp growing in shallow waters. Useful for basic crafting and nutrition in emergencies. Rare specimens have iridescent fronds that tidal researchers pay well for.

**Bioluminescent Algae**
Glowing algae colony clinging to rocks. Harvested for light-emitting compounds used in equipment and trade goods.

**Salt Frond**
Hardy seaweed anchored to shallow rocks, its fronds coated in mineral-rich salt crystals useful for preservation and basic chemistry.

##### Minerals

**Coral Deposit**
Calcium carbonate structure built by tiny organisms. Useful construction material available in abundance throughout the Shallows.

**Tidal Stone**
Smooth stone polished by constant wave action. Contains trace minerals and serves as a reliable, if unglamorous, extraction target.

**Sea Crystal**
Translucent crystalline formation grown in tidal caves. Refracts light beautifully and commands premium prices. Rare specimens show brilliant internal refraction.

##### Artifacts

**Tidal Compass** *(Rare)*
> *"Tracks the gravitational influence of both moons. We don't know how. The Ancients didn't have our moons' orbital data. Or did they?"*
A barnacle-encrusted device that still functions despite apparent millennia of submersion.

#### Ancient Ruins (`ancient_ruins`)

##### Creatures

**Ruin Seeker** -- Predator
> *"Drawn to Ancient technology for reasons xenobiologists cannot explain. The creatures don't use it. They guard it."*
Highly territorial predator that nests near Ancient artifacts. Whether this represents learned behavior, instinct, or something more intentional is a matter of ongoing -- and carefully suppressed -- research.

**Ruin Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Small, quick creature that picks through Ancient refuse. Skittish but will bite if cornered. Their nesting material often contains small artifacts, making Ruin Scavenger dens a low-risk prospecting target.

**Ruin Warden** -- Predator
> *"Large. Territorial. Nests in Ancient structures. Do not enter without a plan for leaving."*
The dominant predator of ruin sites. Fiercely defends territory that happens to coincide with the highest-value archaeological zones. Whether the Wardens are protecting their nests or protecting the ruins is an uncomfortable question.

**Guardian Construct** -- Predator
> *"Not alive. Still defending. Still killing."*
Ancient automaton following defense protocols written millennia ago. Attacks trespassers in ruin sites with mechanical precision. They cannot be reasoned with, intimidated, or redirected. They can, however, be disassembled -- if you survive long enough.

**Relic Beast** -- Omnivore
Mutated creature warped by prolonged ruin exposure. Territorial and far more intelligent than baseline fauna. Some operatives swear the Relic Beasts are getting smarter.

##### Flora

**Phase Bloom**
Anomaly-touched flower existing partially out of phase with normal spacetime. Reality shimmers around rare specimens. Scientific value is considerable; handling without protective equipment is inadvisable.

**Relic Lichen**
Slow-growing lichen found on Ancient construction surfaces. Its metabolic processes incorporate compounds from Prior Inhabitant materials, making it a passive assay tool for ruins archaeology.

##### Minerals

**Anomaly Crystal**
Reality-warped mineral formation requiring extreme caution during extraction. The crystal's internal structure changes depending on the observer's temporal frame. Rare specimens flicker in and out of temporal alignment. Epic-grade formations pulse with quantum instability that equipment sensors cannot reliably measure.

**Ruin Aggregate**
Composite mineral where Ancient construction materials have partially fused with native geology. The resulting material has properties neither component originally possessed.

##### Artifacts

**Ancient Data Core** *(Exotic)*
> *"Intact. Encrypted. We've been trying to crack one for fourteen years. The encryption adapts."*
Storage device from the Prior Inhabitants. Contains data that all four factions want and none have successfully accessed.

**Void-Touched Relic** *(Legendary)*
> *"Radiates energy that makes your teeth itch. Don't hold it too long."*
Object warped by sustained anomaly exposure. The energy signature matches nothing in human physics databases.

### Tier II -- Hazardous Biomes

#### Toxic Wastes (`toxic_wastes`)

##### Creatures

**Toxic Lurker** -- Predator
> *"Invisible in the chemical haze. Your suit's sensors are your eyes here."*
Camouflaged predator that secretes corrosive compounds mirroring the environment's chemistry. The Toxic Wastes' most effective ambush hunter.

**Sludge Grazer** -- Herbivore
Squat, armored creature that filters nutrients from toxic pools. Its shell is heavily corroded but continuously regrows -- a metabolic feat that has attracted considerable pharmaceutical research interest.

**Corrosion Maw** -- Predator
Ambush predator with acid-etched jaws that lurks in chemical pools. Strikes when prey approaches to drink or forage. Its bite introduces compounds that accelerate the Wastes' natural corrosion.

**Fume Drifter** -- Omnivore
Floating scavenger that feeds on chemical vapors and decomposing matter. Releases toxic clouds when threatened -- redundant, given the environment, but effective against filtered operatives whose suits weren't designed for concentrated exposure.

**Acid Maniac** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Corroded beyond recognition. Doesn't eat what it kills. Doesn't stop killing."*
A creature driven to chemical-fueled frenzy by the Wastes' compounds. Its body is heavily corroded, its behavior purely destructive. Helix researchers theorize the Wastes' chemistry triggers a neurological cascade that overrides self-preservation entirely. High threat. High-value chemical reagents on harvest.

##### Flora

**Acid Fern**
Chemically aggressive plant secreting acidic compounds. Thrives in the Wastes by out-corroding its environment.

**Acid Bloom**
Bright yellow flower with petals that secrete contact acids. Rare specimens radiate intense heat from concentrated acid reactions.

**Chemical Bloom**
Resilient bush with a root system that filters and concentrates toxic compounds. Found in both the Toxic Wastes and Miasma Marshes.

##### Minerals

**Corrosive Deposit**
Toxic mineral compound requiring protective equipment for extraction. Rare deposits have unusually concentrated acid cores.

**Acid Stone**
Rock formation with visible acid seepage channels. The mineral has been partially dissolved into useful secondary compounds.

##### Artifacts

**Contaminated Ancient Relic** *(Rare)*
Prior Inhabitant technology saturated with toxic compounds. Purpose unknown, value considerable, handling extremely inadvisable without full chemical protection.

#### Miasma Marshes (`miasma_marshes`)

##### Creatures

**Marsh Lurker** -- Predator
> *"Uses the environment as a weapon. The gas is its hunting ground."*
Toxin-resistant predator that has adapted to weaponize the Marshes' chemical atmosphere. Operatives with compromised filtration are detected and targeted.

**Bog Crawler** -- Herbivore
Slow-moving amphibian grazing on marsh vegetation. Tough hide provides natural acid resistance. Predictable, reliable, and occasionally the only edible thing in the swamp.

**Marsh Snapper** -- Omnivore
Opportunistic feeder lurking in shallow water. Snatches anything small enough to swallow and anything large enough to provoke.

**Miasma Drifter** -- Herbivore
> *"Floats through the gas. Filters nutrients from it. Lives in what kills everything else."*
A buoyant organism that drifts through the toxic atmosphere, filtering nutrients directly from the miasma. Its existence suggests the Marshes' chemistry supports an ecosystem more complex than surveys initially indicated.

##### Flora

**Gas Pod**
Bulbous marsh plant releasing chemical vapors. Rare specimens strain against their own membranes with concentrated marsh gases.

**Marsh Tendril**
Sinuous vine growing from submerged roots. Curls toward motion, suggesting a rudimentary sensory system.

##### Minerals

**Chemical Sump**
Concentrated chemical deposit in the marsh waters. Valuable for industrial chemistry.

**Toxic Crystal Formation** *(Rare)*
Crystallized marsh toxins with unusual chemical properties. Handle with extreme caution.

**Concentrated Gas Vent** *(Epic)*
Pressurized pocket of marsh gases. Rich in volatile compounds, extremely hazardous, and extremely profitable.

##### Artifacts

**Marsh Filtration Unit** *(Rare)*
> *"Ancient atmospheric processor. Half-sunk in sediment. Still filtering after millennia. Better than our equipment."*
A Prior Inhabitant device that raises uncomfortable questions about how long the Marshes have existed -- and whether they're natural.

#### Petrified Expanse (`petrified_expanse`)

##### Creatures

**Dart Runner** -- Predator
> *"Must keep moving. Must keep moving. Must keep moving."*
Swift predator that hunts in bursts between calcification-safe zones. The Petrified Expanse teaches everything the same lesson: stop moving and die. The Dart Runner has internalized this more thoroughly than most.

**Stone Grazer** -- Herbivore
Heavily calcified creature that feeds on mineral-rich lichen. Moves slowly but has armor that makes it functionally invulnerable to most predators. Time is its only real enemy.

**Fossil Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Quick, adaptable creature picking through petrified remains. Strips minerals from fossils with specialized mandibles. Fast enough to avoid calcification. Barely.

**Petrified Lurker** -- Predator
> *"Motionless among the stone trees. You can't tell the difference until it moves."*
Camouflaged ambush predator that remains stationary among petrified formations, waiting for prey to pass within strike range. Its own body shows advanced calcification -- suggesting it spends far longer motionless than should be possible.

##### Flora

**Mobile Vine**
Fast-growing vine that matures before calcification sets in. The Petrified Expanse's most successful plant strategy: be faster than stone. Rare specimens exhibit unusual speed and coordination.

**Stone Moss**
Moss colony growing on petrified surfaces, constantly shedding calcified layers to maintain living tissue underneath.

**Calcite Fern**
Fern with fronds partially mineralized into translucent calcite. The living portions race to grow faster than the stone claims them.

##### Minerals

**Mineralized Log**
Petrified tree trunk preserving ancient organic structures in mineral. The compression of millennia has created materials with unique properties. Rare specimens exhibit unusual mineral replacement patterns.

**Stone Heart**
Dense mineral core found at the center of petrified organisms. The compression of organic-to-mineral transition creates compounds found nowhere else.

##### Artifacts

**Preserved Specimen** *(Rare)*
> *"Perfectly petrified. Every cell intact. The scientific value is incalculable. The ethical questions about disturbing it are uncomfortable."*
Ancient creature caught mid-motion by the petrification process. Found in both the Petrified Expanse and Frozen Reaches, where cold performs the same preservation as stone.

### Tier III -- Hostile Biomes

#### Fungal Depths (`crystal_caves`)

##### Creatures

**Crystal Hunter** -- Predator
> *"Uses light refraction to disorient prey. By the time you see clearly, it's too late."*
Predator with crystalline integument that manipulates the caves' ambient light into disorienting patterns. The most effective visual predator on Terminus.

**Crystal Crawler** -- Herbivore
Slow-moving grazer consuming crystalline lattice-moss. Its shell is composed of the same minerals it eats, making it nearly invisible against cave walls.

**Prism Weaver** -- Omnivore
Uses reflective crystalline structures on its body to scavenge and hunt in the dark cave system. Creates dazzling light displays that serve as both lure and warning.

**Shard Reaper** -- Predator
> *"Blade-like crystalline limbs. Echolocation. The caves' apex predator."*
Apex cave predator with razor-sharp crystalline appendages. Navigates by echolocation in the deep caves where bioluminescence fails.

**Crystal Berserker** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Crystal growths pierce its body from the inside. It's in constant agony. It wants you to share."*
Crystal-fused creature in permanent pain from mineral formations growing through its tissue. The resulting rage is directed at everything within reach. Yields rare crystalline compounds found nowhere else -- the crystal's integration with living tissue creates unique material properties.

##### Flora

**Lattice Moss**
Silicon-based moss spreading across crystal formations. Rare specimens exhibit intricate crystalline patterns throughout.

**Crystal Lichen**
Pale lichen growing in concentric rings around prismatic formations. Under certain light conditions, appears to pulse.

**Prism Bloom**
Flower with translucent crystalline petals that split ambient light into spectral displays. Beautiful and commercially valuable.

##### Minerals

**Prismatic Crystal**
High-purity silicon crystal refracting light into rainbow patterns. Rare specimens are flawless museum-quality pieces. Epic formations project light in directions that defy optical law.

**Cave Geode**
Hollow crystalline formation with spectacular internal structure. Breaking one open is always a gamble -- some contain rare formations, others are empty disappointment.

##### Artifacts

**Crystalline Resonator** *(Epic)*
> *"Ancient technology fused with crystal growth. Still functional. We don't know what function."*
A Prior Inhabitant device that has been partially absorbed by the cave's mineral growth. Despite -- or because of -- this integration, it still operates.

#### Volcanic Reaches (`volcanic_ridge`)

##### Creatures

**Magma Beast** -- Predator
> *"Armored. Slow. Devastating. Do not assume you can outrun it in narrow terrain."*
Armored silicon predator thriving in extreme heat. What it lacks in speed it compensates for with durability that renders most weapons ineffective. The Volcanic Reaches' signature threat.

**Ash Skimmer** -- Omnivore
Heat-adapted flyer gliding on thermal updrafts. Feeds on insects and wind-carried organic matter. Relatively harmless individually; flocks can overwhelm filtration systems with ash displacement.

**Lava Grazer** -- Herbivore
Thick-skinned creature feeding on heat-resistant lichen near lava flows. Virtually impervious to environmental damage and profoundly uninterested in operatives.

**Cinder Stalker** -- Predator
Fast, heat-adapted predator using thermal currents to mask its heat signature from sensors. Strikes from the shimmer of superheated air.

**Ember Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Opportunistic feeder following lava flows, consuming organisms caught in eruptions. Efficient, patient, and always present after geological events.

**Magma Fury** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Molten rock seeps from cracks in its hide. It doesn't slow down. It doesn't cool down."*
Volcanic creature in constant thermal agony. Molten material leaks from fissures in its body as it charges through the landscape. High-value thermal compounds justify the considerable risk of engagement. Helix operatives consider Fury hunts a rite of passage.

##### Flora

**Thermal Vent Moss**
Heat-resistant fungal mat surrounding geothermal vents. Rare patches generate visible heat haze and contain concentrated thermal compounds.

**Magma Bloom**
Heat-tolerant flower rooted in volcanic vents. Its petals glow orange from internal thermal processes. Epic specimens are rooted directly in active vents, producing compounds impossible to synthesize.

**Ash Vine**
Charcoal-dark vine threading through cooled lava formations. Grows toward active thermal sources with observable intent.

##### Minerals

**Volcanic Ore**
Rich metal deposit exposed by volcanic activity. Rare deposits glow with residual magma heat. Epic formations are still actively heated, requiring specialized extraction equipment.

**Obsidian Vein**
Dense vein of volcanic glass running through basalt walls. The edge is sharper than most industrial cutting tools.

##### Artifacts

**Thermal Core** *(Epic)*
> *"Ancient geothermal power source. Still generating heat. After millennia. Our best reactors last decades."*
The Ancients understood thermal energy at a level that makes current human technology look primitive. The Core's power output has not measurably declined since discovery.

#### Crystalline Wastes (`crystalline_wastes`)

##### Creatures

**Crystal Grazer** -- Herbivore
Docile creature subsisting on mineral deposits. Its body is encrusted with the same crystals it consumes, making it part of the landscape in a disturbingly literal sense.

**Null Feeder** -- Herbivore
Crystalline herbivore whose body refracts light, creating visual distortions. Harmless in intention but disorienting in proximity.

**Dimensional Hunter** -- Omnivore
> *"Exists slightly offset from normal space. Hunts by phasing through what you thought was cover."*
Omnivore that exploits the Wastes' dimensional instability to phase through solid matter during hunting. Conventional barriers are not barriers.

**Rift Hunter** -- Predator
Stalks crystalline corridors using reflections to confuse and ambush prey. In an environment of mirrors, it has mastered misdirection.

**Waste Drifter** -- Omnivore
Semi-corporeal entity phasing between crystal formations, feeding on energy residue. Whether it is an animal, a phenomenon, or something else is debated.

**Crystalline Maniac** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Dimensional flux made solid and angry. Attacks from spatial coordinates that shouldn't exist."*
Crystal-warped entity in perpetual dimensional instability. Its attacks originate from shifting spatial positions that make conventional defense strategies unreliable. The Wastes' most dangerous endemic predator.

##### Flora

**Singing Reed**
Tall, thin crystalline plant vibrating at frequencies just below human hearing. The subsonic resonance causes unease in operatives. Rare colonies produce harmonics that cause visual disturbances.

**Lattice Flower**
A flower whose petals are composed of interlocking crystal planes. When observed from different angles, it appears to show different flowers entirely.

**Null Grass**
Crystalline pseudo-grass growing in mineral-rich anomaly zones. Sharp but valuable. Found in both the Crystalline Wastes and Void Rift.

##### Minerals

**Null Stone**
Dense crystalline stone that absorbs energy rather than conducting it. Rare formations actively dampen sound in a sphere around them.

**Phase Mineral**
Mineral deposit existing in multiple temporal states. Extraction is complicated by the fact that the mineral is only partially present at any given moment.

**Resonance Core** *(Epic)*
Dense crystalline core generating audible tones when approached. The tones shift in response to observer movement, suggesting a feedback mechanism.

##### Artifacts

**Echo Record** *(Rare)*
Temporal recording preserved in crystalline matrix. Replays moments from the past with unsettling fidelity.

**Singing Spire** *(Epic)*
> *"Three meters tall. Humming with a complex chord that shifts based on the positions of both moons."*
A slender crystal tower whose harmonic output correlates with celestial mechanics. The Ancients understood Terminus's orbital relationships. The implications are significant.

**Crystal Memory Lattice** *(Epic)*
Flat crystalline formation etched with microscopic patterns encoding information. Not Prior Inhabitant data storage -- something older.

**Dimensional Fragment** *(Exotic)*
A piece of reality torn loose by dimensional instability. Valuable to researchers. Disturbing to everyone else.

#### Frozen Reaches (`frozen_expanse`)

##### Creatures

**Frost Stalker** -- Predator
> *"Marathon hunter. Outlasts everything. The cold is its weapon; patience is its strategy."*
The Frozen Reaches' signature predator. Does not chase prey -- follows it until exhaustion and cold do the work. Operatives report being tracked for hours before contact.

**Ice Burrower** -- Predator
Armored predator that tunnels through permafrost to ambush prey from below. The surface tremors are the only warning. Experienced operatives learn to read the vibrations.

**Frost Grazer** -- Herbivore
Heavily insulated herd animal scraping ice-algae from frozen surfaces. Conserves energy with an efficiency that researchers find remarkable.

**Blizzard Maniac** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Abandoned energy conservation. Charges through blizzards. The cold should have killed it. The cold made it stronger."*
Frost-crazed creature that has rejected the Frozen Reaches' fundamental survival rule. Where every other species conserves energy, the Blizzard Maniac expends it recklessly. The resulting aggression is matched only by its thermal resistance. Yields rare cryo-compounds.

##### Flora

**Ice Algae**
Hardy extremophile photosynthesizing through ice sheets. Rare colonies pulse with bioluminescence visible through translucent ice.

**Frost Lichen**
Slow-growing lichen extracting energy from thermal differentials. Patience measured in decades.

**Cryo Bloom**
Flower preserved in a shell of perpetual ice. The bloom itself is alive and warm at its center. Epic specimens are surrounded by fractal ice patterns extending meters in every direction.

##### Minerals

**Permafrost Shard**
Frozen mineral formation with cold-stable compounds. Rare specimens don't melt -- even under direct heat application.

**Glacial Core**
Dense mineral formation exposed by glacial retreat. Ancient ice still clings to surfaces. Epic formations are shot through with veins of unknown composition.

##### Artifacts

**Frozen Data Archive** *(Epic)*
> *"Perfectly preserved information storage device. Ice-locked for countless years. The data is intact. The language is not."*
Preserved under ice for millennia. The archive's contents remain encrypted in a Prior Inhabitant format that has resisted all decryption attempts.

### Tier IV -- Extreme Biomes

#### Anomaly Zones -- Starfall Crater (`starfall_crater`)

##### Creatures

**Starfall Grazer** -- Herbivore
Alien herbivore feeding on anomaly-mutated vegetation. Docile but unpredictable near dimensional disturbances.

**Crater Stalker** -- Predator
> *"Uses dimensional instability to ambush from directions that shouldn't exist."*
Apex predator adapted to the Crater's warped physics. Exploits spatial distortions for ambush attacks that arrive from angles conventional geometry does not permit.

**Crater Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Void-mutated scavenger with extra limbs granting unsettling agility. Feeds on anomaly-warped matter that no other species can metabolize.

**Anomaly Predator** -- Predator
Apex predator warped by crater anomalies. Hunts in spatial distortion fields, appearing and disappearing at will.

**Starfall Maniac** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Reality warps in its wake. Insane from prolonged anomaly exposure. Still deadly. More deadly."*
Crater creature driven past all behavioral limits by sustained dimensional interference. The reality distortions that accompany it are almost as dangerous as the creature itself.

##### Flora

**Star Lichen**
Cosmic-origin organism clinging to crater walls. Rare colonies glow with anomalous radiation.

**Void Moss**
Dark moss growing in anomaly-warped areas. Its surface occasionally ripples as temporal micro-distortions pass through. Epic colonies visibly distort the air above them.

**Crater Fern**
Fern with fronds at a slight temporal offset -- they move a fraction of a second after you'd expect. A small wrongness that accumulates into deep unease.

##### Minerals

**Cosmic Fragment**
Starsteel ore from the crater impact. Rare specimens show cosmic patterns in the metal grain. Epic fragments warp the air around them and cause instrument malfunction.

**Impact Glass**
Fused mineral created by the starfall's energy. Contains microstructures that suggest the impact was not entirely natural.

#### Anomaly Zones -- Void Rift (`void_rift`)

##### Creatures

**Void Grazer** -- Predator
Hunts through void-warped space. Its movements seem to skip frames, as though parts of its trajectory exist in spaces humans cannot perceive.

**Anomaly Scavenger** -- Omnivore
Feeds on dimensional tears and void residue. Erratic and unpredictable -- its behavior follows logic that doesn't map to any known behavioral model.

**Void Stalker** -- Predator
> *"Partially phased out of reality. Visible only as a shimmer until it strikes."*
The Void Rift's apex predator. Exists in a state of partial dimensional displacement, making it nearly invisible and extremely lethal.

**Dimensional Aberration** -- Maniac (Mini-boss)
> *"Massive. Mad. Attacks everything. Reality breaks around it."*
Entity driven beyond sanity by prolonged dimensional exposure. Attacks from shifting spatial coordinates with a power that suggests it is drawing energy from the Rift itself.

**Corrupted Magma Titan** -- Predator (Apex)

### Verdant Dynamics Gear Line

#### Suits

**Bioweave Exo-Suit** (Common) -- *Standard Issue*
Woven from synthetic cellulose fibers derived from Luminous Canopy specimens. Issued to all Verdant ground personnel. The suit's organic composition provides passive environmental adaptation -- it breathes, in a sense, adjusting porosity to match atmospheric conditions. Unremarkable by faction standards. Considerably more comfortable than anything Helix produces.

**Chloro-Filtration Suit** (Rare) -- *Hazmat Specialist*
Integrates chlorophyll-membrane filtration layers for sustained operations in toxic environments. Deployed primarily in the Miasma Marshes and Toxic Wastes, where standard filters fail within hours. The suit's living membranes regenerate themselves, extending deployment windows from days to weeks. Verdant's first serious demonstration that biology outperforms engineering.

**Symbiont Exo-Frame** (Epic) -- *Bio-Integration Platform*
Advanced bio-integrated frame incorporating symbiotic micro-organisms within the suit's structure. The organisms actively process environmental toxins, repair micro-damage, and provide real-time biochemical feedback. The relationship between suit and wearer becomes genuinely symbiotic at this tier. Verdant's xenobiologists call it "mutualism." The wearers call it "having a partner that eats poison."

**Mycelial Integration Suit** (Exotic) -- *Classified Prototype*
Classified prototype utilizing a mycelial neural network threaded throughout the suit matrix. The mycelium processes environmental data faster than conventional electronics and can interface with Terminus's native fungal networks. Fewer than a hundred exist. The suits reportedly communicate with each other. Verdant's official position is that this is "an instrumentation artifact."

**The Canopy Sovereign** (Legendary) -- *Pinnacle of Bio-Engineering*
Fewer than forty Canopy Sovereign units exist. Each is grown, not manufactured -- cultivated over months from modified Luminous Canopy genetic stock tailored to its wearer's biology. The suit is, for all practical purposes, alive. It heals, it adapts, it learns. Verdant's masterwork and the reason other factions continue to fund their biological research: envy.

**Canopy Warden Frame** (Epic) -- *Off-Archetype: Combat*
> *"Verdant's security division. Because sometimes the research specimens fight back."*
Bio-reactive combat frame designed for perimeter enforcement in hostile biomes. The Canopy Warden exists because Verdant's biological assets attract raiders, and the local fauna does not respect research permits. Combat-oriented in a faction that prefers sustainability -- a concession to the reality that preservation sometimes requires force.

**The Rootbound Sentinel** (Legendary) -- *Off-Archetype: Combat*
> *"The most heavily armed platform in Verdant's arsenal. They claim it's defensive. The railgun disagrees."*
Root-fiber actuators woven through reinforced bio-composite plating. The Rootbound Sentinel is what happens when Verdant's considerable engineering talent is directed toward destructive capability. The result is a combat platform that grows its own ammunition and repairs its own armor. Other factions find it unsettling. That is the point.

### Helix Extraction Gear Line

#### Suits

**Ironclad Exo-Suit** (Common) -- *Standard Issue*
Forge-pressed durasteel plating rated for 12-hour extraction shifts in standard mining conditions. Every Helix operative starts in an Ironclad. Most retire in one too. The suit is simple, heavy, and nearly indestructible at its price point. Comfort was not a design priority. "You can rest when the shift's over."

**Foundry Plating Suit** (Rare) -- *Deep Operations*
Forged at 1,400 degrees in the Foundry Works. Rated for 6-ton compressive loads. The Foundry Plating Suit represents Helix's first serious investment in its operatives: dual-alloy layered plating that distributes impact force across the entire structure. Operators describe wearing it as "being inside a building."

**Tempered Bulwark Frame** (Epic) -- *Reinforced Platform*
Triple-tempered through proprietary metallurgical processes. The Bulwark Frame is designed for environments where standard heavy armor is insufficient -- Volcanic Reaches lava zones, deep-shaft extraction under active seismic conditions, and hostile fauna contact at Tier III and above. Each plate has been stress-tested to failure and rebuilt.

**Anvil Assault Rig** (Exotic) -- *Premier Assault Platform*
Forged from alloys recovered at extraction depths exceeding corporate safety margins. The Anvil is Helix's answer to the question "what if the mining equipment was also the combat equipment?" A devastating assault platform that doubles as an industrial extraction frame. The overlap is not coincidental.

**The Crucible** (Legendary) -- *Pinnacle of Industrial Engineering*
The single most destructive platform Helix Extraction has ever produced. Forged in a reactor breach that killed three metallurgists and produced an alloy that has never been replicated. Fewer than twenty Crucibles exist. Each weighs more than a light vehicle and hits harder. Helix's ultimate statement: in the contest between man and planet, they will not be outmatched.

**Bore Surveyor Frame** (Epic) -- *Off-Archetype: Recon*
> *"Helix scouts. Because someone has to go first into the dark."*
Reconnaissance frame optimized for subsurface exploration and resource mapping in unmapped cavern systems. The Bore Surveyor exists because Helix learned that sending heavy extraction equipment into uncharted territory is expensive. Sending scouts first is cheaper. The scouts' opinion on this calculation is not recorded.

**The Quarry Phantom** (Legendary) -- *Off-Archetype: Recon*
> *"Designed for operators who map routes into places the planet doesn't want them to go."*
The pinnacle of Helix Deep Survey engineering. The Quarry Phantom trades Helix's signature mass for perception and speed -- an unusual combination in a faction that normally solves problems by being heavier than them. Designed for solo deep-cave reconnaissance, it represents Helix's acknowledgment that some problems require finesse. They find this admission distasteful.

### Nexus Frontiers Gear Line

#### Suits

**Spectre Exo-Suit** (Common) -- *Standard Issue*
Lightweight composite suit issued upon clearance verification. The Spectre prioritizes awareness and response time over protection -- a design philosophy that tells you everything about Nexus Frontiers. Every sensor array is calibrated before armor thickness is considered. "If you need the armor, you weren't paying attention."

**Cipher Recon Suit** (Rare) -- *Intelligence Operations*
Integrated signal-intelligence arrays with cipher-grade encryption. The Cipher Recon Suit turns its wearer into a mobile intelligence platform, passively collecting signals data while providing enhanced environmental awareness. Nexus operatives wearing Ciphers in populated areas have been asked to leave. The suit was already recording.

**Lattice Sensor Frame** (Epic) -- *Advanced Sensor Platform*
Utilizes a crystalline lattice network for distributed environmental analysis across multiple detection spectra simultaneously. The Lattice Frame doesn't just detect threats -- it models them, predicting behavioral patterns from environmental data. Nexus considers this "standard situational awareness." Other factions consider it "deeply unsettling."

**Meridian Intelligence Suit** (Exotic) -- *Classified Intelligence Platform*
Integrates predictive threat-analysis algorithms operating at speeds that approach precognition. The Meridian processes environmental data faster than human perception allows, presenting its wearer with threat assessments before conscious recognition occurs. The suit effectively thinks faster than its operator. Nexus sees no issue with this arrangement.

**The Echo Prime** (Legendary) -- *Pinnacle of Intelligence Engineering*
Processes environmental data at speeds that exceed theoretical limits for conventional computing. The Echo Prime's sensor systems appear to receive data from points in the future -- a capability Nexus officially attributes to "advanced predictive modeling" and unofficially attributes to Prior Inhabitant technology integration. Fewer than thirty exist. Their operators report the suit "knows things." Nexus does not elaborate.

**Vector Enforcer Frame** (Epic) -- *Off-Archetype: Assault*
> *"When diplomacy, espionage, and economic pressure all fail. Nexus calls it 'kinetic negotiation.'"*
Rapid-response assault frame for contested sector operations. The Vector Enforcer exists because intelligence operations occasionally require the application of force. Nexus finds this regrettable. Their targets find it considerably worse. Heavy tactical armor in a faction that normally prizes stealth -- an admission that some problems require visibility.

**The Phantom Protocol** (Legendary) -- *Off-Archetype: Assault*
> *"Merges assault capability with intelligence gathering. The last thing you don't see."*
The most lethal platform in Nexus Frontiers' classified arsenal. Combines assault capacity with full intelligence-gathering capability -- it kills you while recording everything about how you died for future tactical analysis. The fact that Nexus built this tells you what their intelligence suggests is coming.

### The Unaffiliated -- Salvaged Gear

> *"We don't have R&D budgets. We have patience, pliers, and a very good understanding of how other people's equipment works."*
> -- "Patch" Maren, independent engineer, Scarred Badlands

#### Suits

**Patchwork Exo-Suit** (Common) -- *Standard Issue*
Assembled from salvaged components across multiple manufacturers. Nothing matches. Everything works. The Patchwork Exo-Suit is the independent operator's first piece of gear and, for many, their most reliable. Corporate suits have design coherence. The Patchwork has character.

**Salvage Runner Suit** (Rare) -- *Scavenger Specialist*
Field-modified salvage suit optimized for extended scavenging operations in unstable zones. Incorporates the best individual components the operator has found, jury-rigged into compatibility through ingenuity and electrical tape. Each Salvage Runner is unique because each one is assembled from a different pile of stolen parts.

**Drifter Field Frame** (Epic) -- *Independent Operator*
Built from the best parts of three different faction designs. The Drifter Frame represents what happens when an independent operator collects high-quality salvage for long enough to be selective. Faction engineers find it offensive. The frame's performance makes their objections academic.

**Reclaimed Operations Suit** (Exotic) -- *Masterwork Salvage*
Integrates salvaged faction technology in combinations no single faction would approve. The Reclaimed Operations Suit is a testament to independent engineering -- each component was designed for a different purpose, a different faction's specifications, a different doctrine. Together, they achieve something none of them intended.

**The Wasteland Sovereign** (Legendary) -- *Independent Masterwork*
Not manufactured -- assembled, over years, from the finest salvaged components an operator can find. No two Wasteland Sovereigns are identical. Each represents its wearer's entire career in salvage, accumulated triumph by triumph. The suit is a biography written in stolen technology.

**Scrounger Reclamation Frame** (Epic) -- *Off-Archetype: Hazmat*
> *"The best salvage is in the worst places. These suits let you get to it."*
Wasteland Reclamation Crew hazmat frame designed for prolonged operations in contaminated zones the corporations have abandoned as unprofitable. The salvage in those zones is excellent precisely because no one else will go there. The Scrounger frame makes the math work.

**The Mongrel** (Legendary) -- *Off-Archetype: Hazmat*
> *"Built from components that should be incompatible. Works anyway. Works better than it should."*
The hardest-wearing hazmat platform outside faction arsenals. Built from components that span every faction's engineering doctrine, held together by an independent engineer's stubborn refusal to accept that certain technologies can't be combined. They can. The Mongrel is the proof.
